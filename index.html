A Month in the Smog: Tracking Singapore’s October Haze Hour by Hour

A 31-day, 744-hour reconstruction of Singapore’s 24-hour Pollutant Standards Index (PSI) across the North, South, East, West, and Central regions—revealing how transboundary smoke built, peaked, and finally cleared from October 1 to October 31.

THE BUSINESS TIMES GRAPHICS DESK | 31 DAYS × 24 HOURS × 5 REGIONS | PEAK: 230 PSI (WEST · OCT 18, 10:00)

October opened in the Moderate band (59–94 PSI) on October 1 before building into a sustained multi-day episode in mid-October. During the acute October 14–18 surge, all five regions crossed decisively into the Unhealthy range (>100 PSI) while Western and Central Singapore breached the Very Unhealthy threshold (200+ PSI)—culminating in a month-high 230 PSI in the West at 10:00 on October 18, shortly after the Central region reached 229 PSI on October 16.

Prevailing winds and diurnal atmospheric mixing created a marked spatial gradient across the island throughout the month. The West (124.9 monthly mean) and Central (121.9) sectors bore the heaviest sustained particulate burden, whereas the North (95.1 mean) consistently registered the lowest exposure. Cleaner regional airflow returned in the final week of October, bringing readings across every district back into Moderate territory and dropping to a month-low 55 PSI in the East on October 30.
